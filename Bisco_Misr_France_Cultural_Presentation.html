Language & Customs

Egypt 🇪🇬

  • Arabic , high-context, indirect communication
  • Hospitality (Diyafa) is a moral obligation
  • Collective decision-making; family elder defers
  • Ramadan rituals drive peak snack consumption
  • Religious permissibility (Halal) is non-negotiable

France 🇫🇷

  • French , pride in language; avoid English-first branding
  • Laïcité: religion kept private; secular public sphere
  • Individual autonomy in purchase choices
  • Meal culture: quality > quantity; goûter (4pm snack)
  • Origin & provenance as cultural signal of trust

Trade & Investment Climate

STEP 01

Classify the product

Biscuits and wafers must be matched to the right CN/TARIC code before duties or trade measures are priced in.

STEP 02

Meet EU food rules

Traceability, ingredients, and food-law compliance travel with the product from factory to French shelf.

STEP 03

Localize for France

Consumer-facing information must be French-first, so packaging is a market-entry cost, not an afterthought.

STEP 04

Use the trade framework well

The EU–Egypt agreement can help unit economics, but only when origin proof and customs paperwork are complete.

Recommendation

Strategic & Financial Path Forward

Phase 1 , Diaspora Channel

Target Egyptian-French consumers first through ethnic grocery chains, specialty importers, and Arabic/French digital ads. This lowers listing risk, builds proof of demand faster, and protects cash while packaging, pricing, and compliance are refined.

Phase 2 , Mainstream Channel

Enter wider French retail only after repeat sales, unit economics, and trade terms are validated. Lead with Egyptian provenance, French-ready packaging, and individual indulgence in selected mainstream chains and world-food aisles.

The opportunity is real , but profitability comes from sequencing the rollout, not rushing it.
